Proofroom

Making AI review feel like a trusted collaborator, not a black box.

Role
Lead UX Designer at Gravitad
When
2024–2026
Platforms
Desktop and mobile
Made with
Figma, Claude, Cursor

Starting point

Video production teams were losing days getting feedback to the right people. Twelve interviews across two studios showed the problem wasn't review quality but access: who needed to see what, and when. I designed three AI features to close that gap: tagging footage, comparing versions, and routing reviews.

Outcome

A review workspace where the AI does the sorting but people stay in charge. It shows how sure it is, and it asks before anything reaches another person. After four rounds of usability testing, editing cycles were 65% faster and task completion reached 92%.

The original product is no longer online, so these screens are recreated and refined from my original designs, rebuilt with Figma, Claude and Cursor. The outcomes above come from the version that shipped.

When the AI acts, and when it asks

Every AI suggestion follows two questions: how sure is it, and would acting reach anyone besides you? The answers decide whether it acts quietly, asks first, or waits.

Seeing what changed between versions

Comparing cuts used to mean watching both versions and taking notes. Now a wipe shows them side by side, and the AI lines up every change on a shared timeline.

Approving from anywhere

Producers approve cuts between meetings and on set. The phone app keeps the same rules as the desktop: the AI's drafts wait for you, and approval tells you what's still open.
